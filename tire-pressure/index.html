How it works

The volume of a tire barely changes, so the gas inside obeys Gay-Lussac's law: absolute pressure is proportional to absolute temperature.

P_abs = P_abs,0 × T / T₀ (T in Rankine or Kelvin)

Your gauge reads pressure above the atmosphere, so in gauge terms:

P_gauge = (air + 14.7) × (T + 459.67) / 527.67 − P_atm(altitude)

Here "air" is the tire's charge, expressed as the gauge reading it would show at 68°F at sea level. Because the slope is (P_gauge + P_atm) / T, the familiar "1 psi per 10°F" rule only holds for a car tire around 35 psi. Higher pressures and colder starting points change faster.

The tire temperature in the simulation trails a target temperature: ambient, plus solar heating when parked in the sun, plus friction heat when driving. Heating takes about 10 minutes, cooling about 35. At 12:20 the driver tops the tire off to the placard pressure while it's still warm, a common mistake. Watch the marker jump to a new isochore, then end the night under placard again.

Altitude lowers the outside pressure, so the same air charge reads higher on the gauge. The whole surface rises when you move the slider.
